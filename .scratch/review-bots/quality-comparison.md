# CodeRabbit vs Greptile: finding-level quality comparison

Repo `elfuerte72/oplati_podpisku`, PRs #30–#118 (2026-06-25 … 2026-07-25). Data pulled 2026-09-17.

## 0. Bottom line

- **The sample is too small to call a winner on quality.** Only 13 PRs were reviewed by both bots. Greptile's free trial ran out after 50 review rounds on #30–#57, and CodeRabbit skipped 15 of those 28 PRs (plan rate limit, PR merged before review, docs path filter). On the 13 shared PRs the distinct real bugs were **CodeRabbit 10, Greptile 7, found by both 4**. Six of CodeRabbit's ten and three of Greptile's seven come from one 16k-line PR (#35). Without #35 it is **4 vs 4**.
- **CodeRabbit gives more findings, and more of everything.** On the same 13 PRs it left 80 findings to Greptile's 33, about 2.4×. Signal share is similar: 75% vs 79% real-bug + valid-improvement. In absolute noise (nitpick + wrong) that is 20 vs 7. The items CodeRabbit puts in its review body instead of inline (nitpick and outside-diff sections, 29 on these PRs) held 1 real bug, itself a copy of an inline finding, plus 13 nitpicks and 2 wrong. Its inline comments alone (51) had 11 real bugs, 4 wrong, 1 nitpick.
- **Severity labels:** Greptile's P1 was right half the time (7 of 14 were real bugs). CodeRabbit's Major hit 13 of 63 (21%), and 6 Majors plus 1 Critical were outright wrong.
- **Coverage, not quality, was the bigger practical difference in this window.** Greptile found 5 more real bugs on PRs CodeRabbit never reviewed (#31/#33, #39, #43 ×2, #44). CodeRabbit found 5 real bugs on #81–#99 after Greptile had stopped. Both gaps come from billing and configuration.
- **Addressed rate** for real bugs is high for both and says more about the author's fix-everything workflow than about the bots. Several fix commits credit a bot by name ("greptile P1", "Greptile P2"). The author also "fixed" several false positives: the Telegram 4096-limit claim that both bots made, plus F148 and F173.

**Top real bugs one bot caught and the other missed (on PRs both reviewed; none explained by review timing):**

CodeRabbit only:
1. #35 F013: referral commission was accrued even when the order's transition to `paid` failed (cancelled/expired order paid late). Money. Fixed in ca1f312.
2. #35 F017: an identity merge turned a referral commission into a self-commission (beneficiary = source) that stayed withdrawable. Money. Only partly fixed (ca1f312; compensation on main in 74f32b2).
3. #57 F131: the post-link payment handoff awaited `confirmOrder` (up to 60 s) inside a 30 s webhook. A slow provider kills the reply, and Telegram's retry reuses the spent token, so the user is told the link is invalid. Fixed in 714bdda.
   (Also: #51 F104/F107 `cardIssueFeeKopecks` never persisted. The fee snapshot was always NULL, though the charged total was correct.)

Greptile only:
1. #35 F044: opening the partner cabinet before chatting created the user without reading the `ref` cookie, so the referral was lost for good (`referred_by` is immutable). CodeRabbit reviewed three commits that contained this. Fixed in 5602d9f.
2. #41 F086/F087: an account merge dropped the web row's active boost and monthly streak, so the partner lost the boosted rate for the month and could miss the serial bonus. Money. Fixed in 413908f.
3. #54 F118: the AI system prompt still told web-chat customers that payment is taken in rubles/USDT by a human operator. There is no USDT path and no operators. Fixed in b26d553.

## 1. What was actually compared (read this first)

- **Greptile reviewed only PRs #30–#57** (28 PRs, 50 review rounds). On #57 it hit "the 50-credit limit for trial accounts"; from #68 every PR got "Your trial has ended". It left zero findings on #58–#118.
- **CodeRabbit produced a review on 27 PRs** in #30–#118 (31 reviews). On the others it posted "Review limit reached" (plan rate limit), "Review failed: the pull request is closed" (author merged within ~1–5 min, before CodeRabbit finished), or "Review skipped due to path filters" (docs-only PRs; `.coderabbit.yaml` excludes `**/*.md` and `docs/**`).
- In Greptile's window #30–#57, CodeRabbit reviewed 13 of 28 PRs: #30, #34, #35, #36, #38, #40, #41, #48, #51, #52, #54, #56, #57. Missing: rate-limited #32 #33 #39 #42 #43 #44 #47 #53; merged before review #31 #45 #49; docs skipped #37 #46 #50 #55.
- So the true head-to-head sample is **13 PRs** where both bots produced a review with findings: the 11 PRs with inline comments from both (#30 #34 #35 #36 #38 #40 #41 #48 #51 #56 #57) plus #52 and #54, where CodeRabbit's findings sit only in the review body (nitpick / outside-diff). CodeRabbit's 51 findings on #63–#117 have no Greptile counterpart and are reported separately.
- Review cadence differs: Greptile re-reviewed on pushes (e.g. 10 rounds on #35, 3 on #40), CodeRabbit reviewed once per PR in most cases (4 reviews on #35, 2 on #92) and then hit its limit. Some non-overlap is caused by this and is marked below.
- The author (`elfuerte72`) never replied to any bot comment. "Addressed" is judged from code at later PR commits, the squash-merge commit and follow-up commits on main — not from replies. CodeRabbit's own "✅ Addressed in commit" markers were checked, not trusted (wrong on F017, F121 (partly), F183; missed on F015/F016).

## 2. Method

1. Fetched for every PR #30–#118: `pulls/<n>/comments`, `pulls/<n>/reviews`, `issues/<n>/comments`, `pulls/<n>/commits`, `pulls/<n>`.
2. Findings extracted:
   - CodeRabbit: every top-level inline comment (89); every item under "🧹 Nitpick comments" (38) and "⚠️ Outside diff range comments" (4) in review bodies. Counts reconciled with the section headers. No "♻️ Duplicate comments" sections existed. "Actionable comments posted: N" = inline count, so not double-counted.
   - Greptile: every inline comment (40, badges P1/P2/security), and every item under "Comments Outside Diff" in the top-level summary. Greptile edits that summary in place on each re-review, so all historical versions were recovered through GraphQL `IssueComment.userContentEdits` and deduplicated by file+title (15 items). Greptile's confidence score is in the summary (4/5 or 5/5 on every PR) and is not a finding.
   - Total: CodeRabbit 131, Greptile 55, 186 findings.
3. Each finding was judged against the code at the commit the bot reviewed (bare clone with `refs/pull/*/head`, since the PR branches were squash-merged). Verdicts: `real-bug` (claimed failure is reachable and causes wrong behavior / money / data / security problem), `valid-improvement` (true, not a bug: hardening, observability, perf, tests, a11y), `nitpick` (style, naming, JSDoc, docs), `wrong` (false positive, misread code, impossible scenario, or harmful recommendation). Every `real-bug` and `wrong` cites file:line at the reviewed commit. Unreachable code (no callers yet) was graded `valid-improvement`, not `real-bug` (F019/F042, F055/F062).
4. Judging was split across six parallel reviewers with one shared rubric; I re-verified a sample of the load-bearing `wrong` verdicts myself against the code (F008, F037, F133, F141, F165) and all held. Borderline calls are listed in §6.
5. Overlap: on the 13 head-to-head PRs, findings were linked when they share a root cause, even on different lines or commits. Same-bot duplicates (inline + outside-diff copy, the same race posted twice, "add a test for bug X") were merged into one issue for the distinct-issue counts. Non-overlap caused by timing (code added after the other bot's last review) is flagged.
